SERI

the Spatial Effective Rainfall Index

A per-event measure of how ecologically effective a rainfall event is in hyper-arid drylands. SERI scores not the scalar intensity of a storm but its spatial coherence — the dimension that classical aridity indices leave out.

SERI = P · Aα · f(season) · g(substrate)
Open the calculator → pip install seri-index How to cite

The idea

Classical indices treat rainfall as a scalar. Drylands don't.

In a hyper-arid landscape, two events of identical station-measured intensity can produce radically different ecological responses. A 15 mm event spread contiguously over ~800 km² recharges wadis and triggers mass germination; the same 15 mm confined to a 50 km² convective cell evaporates within hours and is biologically invisible. SERI makes the pulse-reserve paradigm operationally measurable from satellite data at the event scale, by weighting an event by the contiguous area receiving rainfall above a 5 mm·day⁻¹ threshold.

Beyond the Sahara

Recalibrate SERI for your desert

The framework transfers worldwide; the numerical coefficients are regional. Transferring to another dryland reduces to substituting a regional event archive and recalibrating α and the seasonal / substrate coefficients against locally observed response. We explicitly invite this — it is the natural route to a multi-region, cross-desert formulation of SERI.

Scope. SERI is a concept paper anchored on a single fully-documented event; α = 0.68 is a working value pending full calibration across ≈150 events (companion paper, in preparation). The index complements — does not replace — long-term drought and land-degradation trend metrics, and is directly relevant to land-degradation-neutrality monitoring (UNCCD; SDG indicator 15.3.1).
